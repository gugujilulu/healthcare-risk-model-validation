# Limitations and Non-Claims

- The public dataset is synthetic and cannot reproduce the clinical distribution of the private screening cohort.
- Synthetic figures demonstrate workflow behavior, not clinical performance.
- Curated files under `evidence/` are aggregate context, not the full private evidence package.
- The original independent extension was exploratory; some best-observed held-out choices were informed by held-out comparisons.
- The public reconstruction improves the selection protocol but does not retroactively change how the original exploratory values were obtained.
- Future-period temporal validation is a stronger stress test than random splitting but is still retrospective and within the same healthcare system.
- The project does not establish prospective clinical utility, deployment readiness, treatment benefit, or resource-effectiveness.
- Decision-curve outputs depend on the threshold range, assumed action rule, and probability estimates.
- Subgroup analyses are descriptive and do not establish causal differences or subgroup-specific clinical utility.
- The post-screen triage branch is a separate prediction-time task and should not be interpreted as an improvement to pre-screen/general long-term risk estimation.
- No claim is made that the baseline Mirai image network itself was retrained or fine-tuned.
